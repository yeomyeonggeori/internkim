title: The data room
description: Where the company's documents live, who may open each one, and how to ask internkim for one.

The data room is the company's document archive: the contracts, statements,
minutes, policies and photos that the numbers in the company's tables came
from. A figure lives in a table; the document it came from lives here, and each
names the other. Every member and internkim read from the same archive, so a
document filed once is findable by anyone cleared for it, and one never filed
is missing for everyone.

It is organized as fourteen folders, called domains. Each domain has a
clearance, a number from 0 to 3, and each member has one too. A member opens
any domain at their own clearance or below. That single comparison is the whole
access model; [Clearance](/docs/data-room/clearance) walks through it.

## The seven rules

1. Access follows the document's folder. Files have no separate permission setting.
2. People write a document's label: title, kind, date and one-line summary. The
   index and domain catalogs are generated from those labels and checked against them.
3. Every PDF, image or recording has a text sidecar that describes its contents.
   internkim reads the text and opens the original when the sidecar cannot answer.
4. A new version identifies the document it replaces; earlier versions remain
   marked as superseded.
5. Publish outside material as a copy in the public domain that names its source
   document.
6. Each member has one clearance number. Members start at 1, the first administrator
   holds 3, and administrators can adjust lower-clearance members up to their own.
7. Each document has a record row and a stored file. One policy compares the
   reader's and document's clearance to decide access.

## Using it

Ask internkim, in the messenger or the web app's chat screen, the way you would
ask the colleague who keeps the archive.

- "Find the lease for the Pangyo office." It searches the catalogs you are
  cleared for and answers with the document's title, date and summary, opening
  the file only if the summary is not enough.
- "File this." with an attachment. It derives the text, drafts the label,
  proposes a domain, and asks you to confirm before anything moves.
- "Send me the original." It delivers the file to you, as a message or into
  your own `artifacts/` folder, and logs the delivery under your name.
- "Publish the 2026 deck." An administrator's request. It copies the deck
  into the public domain with its source recorded.

A member sees what they are cleared for, and a search does not mention what
they are not.
