---
title: Asking internkim
description: What to ask for, what it refuses and why, and what the ledger keeps of every request.
---

internkim is the archive's clerk. It starts every session from the company
profile and the index, so it knows which domains exist and what each holds
before you say anything, and it reaches a document by searching the catalogs
you are cleared for. It never lists the whole tree, and it never reads an
original when the sidecar can answer.

## Find [#find]

"Where is the lease for the Pangyo office?" "Which NDAs expire this year?"
"What did the 2025 audit say about revenue?"

It searches the catalogs, narrowed to a domain or a year when the question
names one, and answers from the summaries. When the summary settles the
question, that is the answer, with the document named so you can ask for it.
When it does not, internkim opens the sidecar, then the derived text, then the
original, and says which it read.

A search runs as you. A document above your clearance is absent from the
result, and internkim does not say that something was withheld, because from
where it sits nothing was.

## Add [#add]

"File this," with an attachment, a file in your workspace, or a path in
`tmp/`.

The file is hashed, its kind detected and its text derived, as you and in your
own `tmp/`, so an untrusted file is opened with your permissions and nothing
more. internkim drafts the label, proposes a domain, and shows you the draft.
On your confirmation the document moves into place, the index and the domain's
catalog are regenerated, and the document is registered on the record.

If the domain is above your clearance, the document becomes a submission in
`inbox/`, and internkim tells you who can raise it.

## Publish [#publish]

"Publish the investor deck." "Put the ISO certificate on the public site."

An administrator's request. internkim copies the document into `00-public`,
records who published it, when and from which `id`, and the mirror to the
public share follows. For a redacted edition, the administrator supplies the
edited file and internkim files it as a copy whose `from` still names the
source.

## Check [#check]

"Check the data room."

A pass over the whole archive that reports what is out of shape: a binary
without a sidecar, a label missing a required field, two documents sharing an
`id`, a `domain` that disagrees with the folder, a directory over forty
entries, a stale index or catalog, a submission waiting in `inbox/` more than
seven days, a `supersedes` pointing at nothing, a public document without
`published`, or a published copy whose `from` names nothing. Each item names
the file. Nothing is fixed silently; the report is the result.

## What it refuses [#refusals]

| Asked for | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| a document above your clearance | it is not found | the record hides the row and the file; internkim never held it |
| editing a filed document in place | refused; a new version with `supersedes` is offered | nothing is overwritten, so a reference from last year still resolves |
| copying a file into a domain folder by hand | refused; filing through internkim is offered | a file added outside ingest leaves the catalog out of step with the directory |
| raising your own clearance | refused | only an administrator above you can, and nobody raises a peer |
| lowering yourself below 3 as the last member at 3 | refused | the company would have nobody who can read governance |
| publishing, as a non-administrator | refused; the request is passed to an administrator | placing a copy in `00-public` is an administrator's act |
| delivering an original to someone else | refused; delivered to you instead | an original goes only to the requester; passing it on is your own act, logged as yours |
| a number from a document | answered from the metric tables, with the document named as evidence | a figure lives in a table and the data room holds what it rests on |

## What the ledger records [#ledger]

Every request through internkim is a task on the record, and the ledger names
each document that task touched, by `id`, with who asked and when. A filing, a
raise, a publication and a delivery are all tasks, so each has an entry. The
ledger is what a company consults when it asks who saw a document and who sent
it where, and it is the reason an original is handed only to the person who
asked for it. A member reading through internkim leaves a trail; a member with
the file in hand is trusted, and the trail says where the trust was placed.
