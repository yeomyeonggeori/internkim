---
title: Access and sharing
description: Circles, invitations and publication.
---

A company member reads the data room through the circles they belong to. A
circle names `readableCategories`, an array of codes. `F` includes all finance
children, including ones added later. `FS` grants financial statements alone.
Belonging to several circles combines them; there are no deny overrides. A
circle grants reading. Company administration and document editing are
separate permissions. Filing eligibility does not limit a circle's scope:
both parent and intermediate codes can appear in one.

| readableCategories | Access |
|---|---|
| `["F"]` | Finance and every current or future child |
| `["FS"]` | Financial statements only |
| `["FS", "FP"]` | Financial statements and payroll |

A category without children can hold documents. A category with children
groups their documents and remains a valid scope.

A circle is also a shared folder and a shared memory on the company host. Its
`id` is the lowercase name the host uses for both, such as `leadership`, and
its name is what people read. Every new member joins `member`; administrators
created with a company also join `leadership`. Later changes to administrator
status change no circle. Settings → Company places each member in circles and
edits what each circle reads.

Editing a circle immediately changes what its members and every share of it
can read. Adding a child immediately extends existing parent scopes. Review
those readers before changing either. Category codes and ancestry are stable.

## Sharing outside the company

An administrator lends what one circle reads to an external email or an
explicitly public audience. A verified email recipient must accept the
invitation and gains no company membership, circle or host filesystem
identity. An expired or revoked share stops new reads. Original file downloads
require `canDownload`; text previews follow read access. A downloaded copy
cannot be recalled, and issued file links expire within ten minutes.

Publication requires an explicit public share. It never publishes `X`.

## Code-protected links

An active member creates a link by choosing a circle within their own access.
Every circle is eligible, including leadership; `X` follows the same circle and
creator permissions. Its default lifetime is three days; the available
lifetimes are defined by
[`dataRoomLinkLifetimeHours`](https://github.com/yeomyeonggeori/internkim/blob/main/web/src/lib/data-room/links.ts).
The database enforces a seven-day ceiling.

The app generates a six digit code, displays it once and stores its salted
password hash. The recipient enters the code and acknowledges the versioned
confidentiality notice. This creates a guest session for up to one hour,
bounded by the link's expiration, without creating a company member. Ten code
attempts per network address in fifteen minutes temporarily lock further
attempts. A link also has a shared ceiling of one hundred attempts per window.

Every database read checks the link, what its circle reads now and the
creator's current access. Widening the circle cannot exceed the creator's
access. The creator or a company administrator can revoke a link. An open page
loses further access and clears its displayed documents on its next session
check. Original download URLs expire within one minute. Already downloaded
files cannot be recalled.

The confidentiality acknowledgment records its notice version and time; any
separate confidentiality agreement remains the company's responsibility.

Moving a document into another category changes its readers.
