---
title: Clearance
description: Four numbers, who sets whose, and how a document crosses from one to another.
---

Every member has a clearance and every domain has one. A member opens every
domain at their clearance or below. The comparison is made by the company
record, on each row and each stored file, so a document above a reader's
clearance is absent for them: a search does not list it, a tool does not open
it, and a script they run finds nothing.

| Clearance | Reader | Domains |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | anyone outside the company | `00-public`, mirrored to the public share |
| 1 | every member | corporate, people, ip, product, marketing, operations, compliance, media |
| 2 | management | finance, contracts, fundraising |
| 3 | representative, board | governance, hr-records |

## Who sets whose [#who-sets-whose]

A new member starts at 1. The first administrator of a company holds 3, and
promoting someone to administrator sets their clearance to the promoter's.

An administrator may change the clearance of a member who is currently below
them, to any value up to their own. Three requests are refused however they
are phrased: raising a peer or a superior, a non-administrator setting anyone's
clearance, and an administrator lowering themself when it would leave the
company without a member at 3.

Clearance is one number per person. It says nothing about which projects or
conversations someone is part of; that is a circle, and it lives in the
messenger and the workspace. A document meant for one circle belongs in that
circle's workspace folder rather than the data room.

## Adding a document [#adding]

Every member may add. Three rules decide what happens.

1. **Into a domain at or below your clearance, the document is filed
   directly.** internkim drafts the label, you confirm, and it is in place.
2. **Into a domain above your clearance, the document is a submission.** It is
   registered at your own clearance with the intended domain written on it,
   and waits in `inbox/` until a member cleared for that domain raises it. An
   accountant at clearance 1 filing an audit report into finance does exactly
   this, and the finance manager's confirmation is the raise.
3. **Moving, superseding or handing out a document requires reading it.** The
   member who moves a document between domains, marks it superseded, or asks
   for the original must be cleared for it.

A submission older than seven days in `inbox/` is flagged by the data room's
check, so a raise nobody made is noticed.

## Publishing to `00-public` [#publishing]

A published document is a copy in `00-public`, made by an administrator, with
`published.from` naming the source and the date and person recorded. The source
stays where it was, at its own clearance. The public domain is mirrored to the
company's public share, which is what a visitor or the company site reads.

A copy whose contents equal the source is the source published. A copy whose
contents differ is a redacted edition: the same document with a section removed
for the outside, still naming what it came from. A public document without a
`published` field, or one whose `from` names nothing, fails the data room's
check.

## Originals [#originals]

Reading a summary is one thing; holding the file is another. An original is
delivered only to the person who asked, as a message to them or into their own
`artifacts/` folder. Posting it on from there, to a channel or to someone
outside, is their own act and is logged as one. No system can stop a cleared
person from passing on what they read; the ledger, which names each document a
task touched, is what answers the question afterwards.

## An external data room [#external]

An investor, a law firm or an acquirer doing due diligence receives an export:
chosen documents copied to a separate share opened for them. The live data
room stays member-only, and the export is a snapshot that does not change when
the archive does.
